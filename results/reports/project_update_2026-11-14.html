Project update and plan to the final presentation

Where we stand on 14 November 2026, what we have agreed, where the code and the agreed method have drifted apart, and a week-by-week plan to the final presentation on 17 / 18 December.

Sources: Granola notes from our class sessions (10 Sep, 17 Sep, 15 Oct, 22 Oct, 5 Nov) and the GitHub repo yainge/bess-opportunity-map-ddc at commit 0eb90e0 (2 Nov). There are no notes on this project after 5 Nov, so anything later is marked “to confirm”.

In one paragraph

We are building a site-selection filter for battery storage developers in Catalonia: a map that scores locations on grid connection readiness, congestion, demand, renewables and land, and highlights candidate sites. The data layer is largely done: 117 REE substations, renewables, municipal demand and all GIS layers are in the pipeline and produce rankings, an ML check and sensitivity figures. The main gaps are congestion (ERNI) data, the scoring weights (still placeholders), and the fact that the method we agreed on 5 Nov (a heat map of scored points plus node clustering) is not what the code currently produces (a ranked list of nodes). With about five weeks left, the priority is to freeze the method this week, close that gap, and spend December on validation, the business case and the presentation.

Agreed method vs. what the code does

This is the most important finding of this update. The pipeline was built before the 5 Nov decisions, and several of them aren't in it yet.

These two views fit together. Node scores can drive the heat map: each map point inherits the score of its nearest available node, discounted by distance. So we don't have to choose one; we have to build the surface on top of what exists.

Decisions we need this week

1 · How do the heat map and the node ranking fit together?

Recommendation: keep nodes as the scoring unit (it's built and defensible) and generate the heat map as a 0.01° point grid. Each point gets the score of its nearest available node, reduced by distance, then masked by land suitability. That delivers the agreed visual without redoing the data work.

2 · Congestion: get ERNI, or drop it with a stated limitation?

Recommendation: give Hsiang Wei until 21 Nov to extract the top-30 node values from the REE PDFs. If that isn't possible, drop ERNI from the scoring and state it clearly as a limitation; the method already handles missing indicators.

3 · Which clustering input do we use?

Recommendation: decide from Yuki's three-way test (node coordinates, scattered points, 0.01° grid). Pick the option whose clusters are contiguous and easiest to explain. Use Fernando's line data only to check feasibility, with the stated assumption that unconnected nodes link to the nearest node.

4 · What happens to the ML classifier?

Recommendation: keep it as supporting evidence, not the main method. It shows that our indicators line up with real developer choices (AUC 0.90). It answers the professor's call for a data-driven element without the scoring depending on it. Confirm with Ferran, since the 22 Oct sign-off isn't recorded.

5 · Scope cut: level 3 (self-consumption)

Recommendation: drop it from the deliverable and mention it as future work. Five weeks isn't enough to do it well alongside the business case.

6 · Name an integration coordinator

Recommendation: one person merges branches into main and owns the final run that produces every figure in the presentation.

Risks and open questions

  • Intermediate presentation feedback is unknown. If Ferran or the professor redirected us, this plan needs adjusting before 19 Nov.
  • Congestion data may never be usable. Plan the story so it still works without ERNI.
  • Weights will be challenged. Ground them in a short rationale (developer interviews, literature or a stated assumption) and show the sensitivity results next to the map.
  • The ML labels describe renewable siting, not BESS siting. Present the classifier as a consistency check, not a prediction of where batteries should go.
  • Demand provenance is still unverified. Record the ICAEN source URL and publication date before the final report.
  • Optional validation lead. At the Siemens session on 10 Nov, Sincal's hosting-capacity analysis was suggested as relevant, with possible academic licensing. It's only worth pursuing if there is spare time after 6 Dec.
